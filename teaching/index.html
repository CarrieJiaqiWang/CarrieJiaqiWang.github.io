Teaching

Making mathematical reasoning visible.

My goal is to help students formulate a question, develop an argument, and judge what its conclusion means.

Teaching approachCourse experienceTeaching development
01

Scaffold productive struggle

I begin with accessible examples and build toward more demanding problems. In a graduate recitation on coupling, a construction using two biased coins gave students a shared starting point before we moved to Poisson approximation.

02

Ask students to explain

Individual thinking followed by peer discussion gives students time to develop an idea. Their explanations make misconceptions visible and help me decide whether to continue, pause, or introduce a different example.

03

Make feedback usable

I connect feedback to a next attempt. Detailed rubrics and comments identify specific gaps; a related problem with less guidance helps students practice and assess their developing understanding.

Teaching interests

Probability, computation, and decisions.

I am interested in undergraduate and graduate probability, stochastic processes, and probabilistic modeling, including their connections to statistics and data science. My experience also prepares me to teach linear algebra, optimization, calculus, and analysis.

Two proposed future electives are Stochastic Processes and Sampling Algorithms and Modeling Extremes and Risk. These would connect mathematical foundations with simulation, model assessment, and decisions under uncertainty.

AI and mathematical learning

I see a role for AI in exploring examples and developing simulations while students remain responsible for their assumptions, reasoning, and conclusions. In foundational courses, I would pair clear guidance on permitted use with independent attempts, staged hints, and opportunities to explain an argument.
