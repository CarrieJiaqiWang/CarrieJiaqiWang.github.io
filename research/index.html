Research

How do extreme episodes unfold?

My work develops probability theory for rare events under dependence, with the goal of understanding their mechanisms, likelihood, and consequences.

Research directionsPublicationsSelected talks
01

Heavy tails and extreme-event clusters

With Gennady Samorodnitsky · Cornell University

One exceptional input can influence many neighboring observations. In joint work on multivariate moving averages, we characterize how this common cause creates clusters of large deviations in short- and long-memory settings. Conditional point-process limits describe the dominant shock's location and effect, together with the forward and backward extents of the extreme cluster.

My current questions include how these mechanisms change across tail regimes and which features of dependence determine persistence and recurrence.

02

Rare trajectories and multiple shocks

Current theoretical work

Some risks concern a sequence of events: losses in adjacent periods, repeated shortfalls, or a disruption followed by slow recovery. I study sample-path large deviations for dependent heavy-tailed processes, including trajectories that require several shocks acting together.

A central question is which details of the response a model must retain. Similar limiting aggregate paths can conceal different rare-event probabilities when a single shock is spread across observations.

03

Learning and simulating power-grid outages

With Daniel Adrian Maldonado · Argonne National Laboratory

I analyze outage records, lightning measurements, and network structure to study patterns of joint failures. I developed a network sampler that generates an underlying cluster, then selects failed lines through thinning that penalizes high degrees. Comparisons examine component sizes, connectivity, and degree patterns against observed outage networks.

The next step is to combine individual line risks with joint-failure structure in scenarios conditioned on weather, assessing topology, geographic extent, and extreme cluster sizes.

Earlier work and broader directions

With Ziv Goldfeld, I developed a differentially private Sinkhorn algorithm with convergence and privacy guarantees. With Katya Scheinberg, I studied adaptive derivative-free optimization algorithms. My undergraduate research with Jianwei Huang addressed vehicle-to-grid pricing through Stackelberg games.

Longer-term interests include congestion and recovery in queues, random amplification and feedback, and the effects of extreme dependent noise on stochastic optimization.
